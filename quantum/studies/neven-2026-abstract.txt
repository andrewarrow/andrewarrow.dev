Hartmut Neven — The Science of Consciousness, 2026
Towards a Brain-Quantum Computer Interface: Identifying Potential Coupling Mechanisms via Anesthetic Isotope Effects
Source: https://tsc2026.org/abstracts_html/hartmut-neven.html

A rigorous test of a theory of consciousness requires demonstrating the ability to predictably alter subjective experience—for instance, by increasing the number of bits experienced per unit of time or by inducing specific qualia. To test our hypothesis that conscious experience results from the formation of quantum superpositions, we propose to coherently link quantum degrees of freedom in a brain to a quantum processor. Recognizing the formidable technological challenges of engineering such an interface, we first seek to identify quantum degrees of freedom suitable for coupling. To this end, we study the effects of anesthetics that reversibly quench consciousness. We report large and statistically significant isotope effects in the behavior of fruit flies (Drosophila melanogaster) exposed to isotopologues of nitrous oxide and isotopes of xenon. We complement these behavioral findings with NMR and electrophysiological studies using human brain organoids, laying the groundwork for future brain-quantum computer interfaces.
